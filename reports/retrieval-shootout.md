## Gates

- `lexical`: query-token recall >= 0.2 and >= 2 shared tokens.
- `hashed_vector`: cosine above the 95th percentile of a single-shared-token null (hashed-vector-single-token-null-v1).
- `graph_expand`: lexical seeds that pass the lexical gate, plus one-hop [[wiki]] neighbours.

Query times are medians over 25 runs with the index already built. The hashed_vector cutoff is computed once per question before timing (mean 16.3392 ms) and is not in either column.

## What the 2026-08-23 table measured

The earlier table is kept unchanged at `reports/history/2026-08-23-retrieval-shootout.json` and its Markdown twin.

- hashed_vector was gated by the lexical recall cutoff (0.2) applied to raw cosine, with no two-token minimum. Its 0/1 abstain measured that scale mismatch, not retrieval quality.
- graph_expand had no gate and always returned up to top_k notes, so its 0/1 abstain could not have been anything else.
- No latency was query-only. Every method redid per-note work for every question: lexical and graph_expand tokenized each note, and hashed_vector rebuilt its index, the most expensive of the three. Query-only, hashed_vector is still the slowest here.

## Limitations

- n is the Harbor fixture set, not a public IR benchmark.
- hashed_vector is a sha256 bag-of-tokens index, not a neural embedding API.
- graph_expand is one-hop [[wiki]] following, not Microsoft GraphRAG.
- Each gate is defined on its own method's scale; abstain counts compare gates, not one shared threshold.
- Latencies are microsecond-scale medians on one machine; they order methods loosely at best.
- Do not generalize these numbers to production corpora.
